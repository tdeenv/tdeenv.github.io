Sydney CBD in 3D: NABERS Ratings

2026

This one is an experiment. I wanted to see how far I could get with AI to clean and visualise sustainability data at a building level.

The map visualises NABERS (the National Australian Built Environment Rating System). It rates commercial buildings out of six stars for energy, water, waste and indoor environment. The ratings are public, but the register is one big spreadsheet of certificates.

Why this matters beyond the map

The 3D map is the eye-catching part, but the underlying data is more interesting. Only a small share of buildings have a rating, and they're almost all big office towers. That's expected, since mandatory disclosure under the Commercial Building Disclosure program covers large office spaces.

Energy is by far the most common rating, with a median of 5 stars, and many of the city's landmark towers sit at 5 to 5.5, including Salesforce Tower, the MLC Centre and Tower 1 Barangaroo. Waste and indoor environment ratings are much less common since they're voluntary, and the buildings that do get them often score very well, with 6 stars the most common waste rating.

Buildings rated below 5 stars could face either a retrofit bill or a shrinking pool of tenants as tenant and government leasing policies push towards higher minimum ratings. Large towers with no current rating are worth a look too, since disclosure is usually only triggered when space is sold or leased.

The pipeline

The project is four short Python scripts that run in order:

  • Clean the register: reshape the NABERS certificate export into one row per building, keeping the best current rating of each type. Then filter it to Greater Sydney by postcode (pandas).
  • Fetch buildings: download building footprints and heights for the CBD from OpenStreetMap (osmnx).
  • Join: match each NABERS record to the building footprint it belongs to (geopandas).
  • Render: extrude the footprints to their heights and export a single interactive HTML file with an Energy / Water / Waste / Indoor toggle (pydeck).

On using AI for this

Using Claude Code meant I could have a working prototype ready in a single afternoon. While not perfect, vibecoding this meant I could iterate quickly using libraries like pydeck which I haven't used before.

Where I'd take it next

I would really like to make the map more interactive, and add the ability to compare portfolios based on the entities that own each building.
